Combat in D&D: how a turn works (2024 rules)

Combat step by step: initiative, what you can do on your turn, how attacks work, cover, damage, Hit Points and what happens when you drop to 0.

Initiative · Your turn · Actions · Attacks · Cover · Damage and HP · 0 Hit Points

Initiative and rounds

When combat starts, everyone rolls initiative — a Dexterity check. Combatants act from highest to lowest; the DM rolls once for a group of identical monsters. A round is everyone’s turns, about 6 seconds of game time.

Surprise: a combatant caught unawares rolls initiative with Disadvantage.

What you can do on your turn

Movement
Up to your Speed (usually 30 feet = 6 squares). You can split it: move, attack, move again.
Action
One action: attack, cast a spell, Dash and so on — see the list below.
Bonus Action
Only if a feature, spell or feat gives you one (such as an off-hand attack or Healing Word). One per turn.
Reaction
An instant response to a trigger, even on another creature’s turn — such as an Opportunity Attack. One until the start of your next turn.
Small things
Opening a door, drawing a weapon, shouting a few words — free, one object interaction per turn.

Actions in combat

Each action is detailed on the Glossary.

Attack
An attack with a weapon or an Unarmed Strike. Extra Attack (level 5 for martial classes) gives two attacks with one action.
Magic
Cast a spell with a casting time of an action, or use a magic item.
Dash
Extra movement equal to your Speed this turn.
Disengage
Your movement doesn’t provoke Opportunity Attacks for the rest of the turn.
Dodge
Until your next turn, attacks against you have Disadvantage (if you can see the attacker) and you make Dexterity saves with Advantage.
Help
An ally gets Advantage on their next ability check, or on their next attack against a foe next to you.
Hide
A DC 15 Dexterity (Stealth) check while out of sight: on a success you’re Invisible until you make noise, attack or are found.
Ready
Set an action to trigger on a condition (“I attack if it comes through the door”) — it happens as a Reaction.
Search
A Wisdom check to find a hidden creature or object.
Study
An Intelligence check to recall what you know about a monster, spell or puzzle.
Influence
Persuade, intimidate or deceive a creature mid-combat with a Charisma or Wisdom check.
Use an Object
Drink a potion, work a complex lock — anything that takes its own action.

Making an attack

Roll a d20 and add your ability modifier and proficiency bonus. If the total equals or beats the target’s Armor Class, you hit and roll damage. A natural 20 always hits and is a Critical Hit: roll the damage dice twice. A natural 1 always misses.

Advantage and Disadvantage
Roll the d20 twice and take the higher (Advantage) or lower (Disadvantage). Multiple sources don’t stack, and having both cancels them out.
Ranged attacks
Weapons have two ranges: at long range you have Disadvantage, beyond it you can’t attack. With an enemy within 5 feet who can see you, ranged attacks have Disadvantage.
Opportunity Attack
When an enemy leaves your reach, you can use your Reaction to strike it once. Disengage and teleportation don’t provoke this.
Grapple and Shove
An Unarmed Strike option: the target makes a Strength or Dexterity save (DC 8 + your Strength modifier + proficiency bonus). A grappled creature can’t move; a shove knocks it prone or pushes it 5 feet.

Cover

Half cover
+2 to AC and Dexterity saves: a low wall, another creature, furniture.
Three-quarters cover
+5 to AC and Dexterity saves: an arrow slit, a thick tree trunk.
Total cover
The target can’t be targeted directly by an attack or a spell.

Damage, Hit Points and healing

Hit Points are your staying power; damage reduces them, healing and rest restore them (up to your maximum). Resistance to a damage type halves it, Vulnerability doubles it, Immunity negates it. Temporary Hit Points are lost first, don’t stack with other temporary HP and fade after a rest.

Dropping to 0 Hit Points

If damage leaves you with negative HP equal to your Hit Point maximum, you die instantly. Otherwise you fall unconscious and make a Death Saving Throw at the start of each of your turns: d20, 10 or higher succeeds. Three successes — you’re stable; three failures — you die. A 1 counts as two failures; a 20 brings you back with 1 HP. Taking damage while at 0 HP is a failure (two on a Critical Hit).

You can stabilize a dying creature with healing or with the Help action and a DC 10 Wisdom (Medicine) check. A stable creature regains 1 HP after 1d4 hours.

Conditions (Prone, Grappled, Frightened and others) are on the Glossary page.
