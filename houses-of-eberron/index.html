Dragonmarked Houses of Eberron: marks, trades and the House Agent

The thirteen Dragonmarked Houses of Eberron: the dragonmark each bears, its trade, its symbol and the tools of the House Agent background. Based on Eberron: Rising from the Last War.

Houses · Marks in the builder · House Agent · House Tarkanan and House Vol

What the Dragonmarked Houses are

The Dragonmarked Houses are large families of a single people whose members sometimes manifest dragonmarks, magical signs on the skin. Each House holds a near monopoly on its trade — transport, healing, communication, security — and stays out of the disputes between the nations of Khorvaire. Not everyone has a mark: the House employs unmarked relatives and outsiders who hope to make a name for themselves in its service.

Dragonmarks in the builder

A character with a dragonmark is a variant of a race: the mark replaces the race’s usual bonuses with its own traits, grants the mark’s spells and expands a spellcaster’s spell lists.

  • Humans: Human (Mark of Making), Human (Mark of Passage), Human (Mark of Sentinel), Human (Mark of Handling); humans and half-orcs — Mark of Finding.
  • Half-elves: Half-Elf (Mark of Detection) and Half-Elf (Mark of Storm).
  • Subraces: halfling — the Marks of Healing and Hospitality; elf — the Mark of Shadow; gnome — the Mark of Scribing; dwarf — the Mark of Warding.

The House Agent background

The agent has sworn loyalty to one of the Houses: they gather information and carry out missions for it. If they have a mark, they are most likely a member of one of the House’s families; if not, an outsider hoping to advance in its service. The background grants Investigation and Persuasion and two tools from the table above; in their House’s territory the agent can always find food and lodging for themselves and their companions.

The agent’s roles from the book’s table: acquisition, investigation, research and development, security, intimidation, exploration, negotiation, covert operations.

House Tarkanan and House Vol

Besides the thirteen Houses, there is House Tarkanan — a fellowship of aberrant dragonmark bearers whom the other Houses don’t recognize — and the long-destroyed elven House Vol, which bore the Mark of Death.

Dragonmarks in the 2024 rules

In Eberron: Forge of the Artificer, dragonmarks work differently from the 2014 rules. A mark used to be a species trait: only a halfling could bear the Mark of Healing, for example. Now a character of any species can bear any mark — through a Dragonmark feat.

Where your mark comes from

Dragonmarked heir
You belong to a dragonmarked house’s family. The easiest way is your house’s heir background: instead of an origin feat, it grants the house’s Dragonmark feat at level 1.
Distant offshoot
You come from a distant branch of the family line with no ties to the house. Take the Dragonmark feat at level 4 or later.
Mark of Prophecy
Your mark has nothing to do with your species or family — say, the Mark of Healing on someone who isn’t a halfling. The house might never claim you.
Aberrant mark
A rare aberrant mark that appears in all species and disturbs the houses. It has its own background, Aberrant Heir.

Backgrounds and feats

Heir backgrounds: House Cannith Heir, House Deneith Heir, House Ghallanda Heir, House Jorasco Heir, House Kundarak Heir, House Lyrandar Heir, House Medani Heir, House Orien Heir, House Phiarlan Heir, House Sivis Heir, House Tharashk Heir, House Thuranni Heir, House Vadalis Heir. A house agent without a mark — House Agent.

Dragonmark feats: Mark of Detection, Mark of Finding, Mark of Handling, Mark of Healing, Mark of Hospitality, Mark of Making, Mark of Passage, Mark of Scribing, Mark of Sentinel, Mark of Shadow, Mark of Storm, Mark of Warding. Eberron campaigns also have greater marks (from level 4, if you already have the matching mark) and the Boon of Siberys.

Source: Eberron: Forge of the Artificer, chapter 2.
