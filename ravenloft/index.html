Ravenloft: the Domains of Dread

What Ravenloft is, how the Domains of Dread work and who the Darklords are — based on Ravenloft: The Horrors Within for the 2024 rules. For anyone about to play a horror campaign.

What it is · The Mists · Your character · Domains · Other domains

What is Ravenloft

Ravenloft is D&D’s world of horror. It isn’t one country but many separate islands of nightmare — the Domains of Dread — drifting in the Mists at the edge of the Shadowfell. It takes its name from Castle Ravenloft, home of the vampire Strahd von Zarovich.

Each domain is created by the mysterious Dark Powers around a single villain — its Darklord. The domain is both their realm and their prison: there they are nearly all-powerful, but they can’t leave and relive their own nightmare forever. The inhabitants live under their rule and usually don’t even know other worlds lie beyond the Mists.

The Mists and borders

The Mists surround every domain and can draw in travelers from any world — this is how heroes usually arrive in Ravenloft. A Darklord can close their domain’s borders, making the Mists deadly. Travel between domains relies on Mist talismans — objects tied to a particular domain — and on the cards of the tarokka fortune-telling deck.

A character for Ravenloft

Any character works in a horror campaign, but the book offers options that capture the mood. Species: Dhampir, Hexblood, Lupin, Reborn. Backgrounds: Haunted One, Investigator, Mist Wanderer, Spirit Medium.

Ravenloft’s signature option is the Dark Gift: feats that grant supernatural power at a terrible price (each gift has a drawback, often triggered when you roll a 1 on the d20). You take one instead of an origin feat with the DM’s permission, and the DM can bestow one as part of the story. For example, Living Shadow, Mist Walker, Touch of Death.

Agree on limits with your group: which fears are off the table and how to pause a scene that gets too intense. Horror should scare the characters, not ruin the evening for the players.

Other domains

No one knows how many domains drift through the Mists. The book briefly describes more of them — a DM can develop them or create their own domain using chapter 4: The Agency, Bluetspur, Carnival, Cyre 1313, the Mourning Rail, Forlorn, Ghastria, G’henna, I’Cath, Invidia, Kalidnay, Keening, Klorr, Markovia, Nightmare Lands, Niranjan, Nova Vaasa, Odaire, Richemulot, Rider’s Bridge, Risibilos, Scaena, Sea of Sorrows.

Source: Ravenloft: The Horrors Within, chapters 1 and 2.
