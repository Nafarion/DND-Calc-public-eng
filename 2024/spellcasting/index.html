How spellcasting works in D&D (2024 rules)

What every spellcaster needs to know: spell levels and slots, cantrips, preparing spells, casting time, components, Concentration, rituals, saving throws and spell attacks.

Levels and slots

Every spell has a level from 0 to 9. Level 0 spells are cantrips: you can cast them at will. Other spells need a spell slot of the spell’s level or higher; slots are expended and come back after a Long Rest (a warlock’s Pact Magic slots after a Short Rest). A spell cast with a higher slot is often stronger.

Which spells you can cast

In the 2024 rules every spellcaster has a list of prepared spells; the number is in the class table. After a Long Rest a cleric, druid or wizard can change any number of them, a paladin or ranger one; a bard, warlock or sorcerer changes one when gaining a level. Always-prepared spells (from a subclass or feat) don’t count against that number.

Casting a spell

Casting time
Usually an action (Magic); some take a Bonus Action or Reaction. You can expend only one spell slot per turn.
Components
V — verbal (speak words), S — somatic (a free hand for gestures), M — material (an object; a focus or component pouch replaces it unless it has a cost).
Duration
Instantaneous, for a time (a minute, an hour...) or until dispelled.
Armor
You can cast spells in armor only if you’re proficient (trained) with it.

Concentration

Many lasting spells require Concentration. You can concentrate on only one at a time. When you take damage, make a Constitution saving throw: DC 10 or half the damage, whichever is higher (up to 30); on a failure the spell ends. Having the Incapacitated condition also ends it. More →

Rituals

A prepared spell with the Ritual tag can be cast 10 minutes slower without expending a slot. Any spellcaster can do this.

Attacks, saves and areas

Save DC
8 + proficiency bonus + spellcasting ability modifier. The target makes the save named in the spell; success usually halves damage or negates the effect.
Spell attack
d20 + proficiency bonus + spellcasting ability modifier vs. the target’s AC.
Areas
Cone, cube, cylinder, line, sphere and emanation (an area around a creature that moves with it).
Combining effects
The same spell doesn’t stack on a target: only the most potent instance applies.
