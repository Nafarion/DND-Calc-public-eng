Alignment in D&D 2024: the nine alignments

Alignment is a short description of a character’s moral compass. What each of the nine means in the 2024 Player’s Handbook. What changed between the editions: Alignment: 2014 vs 2024.

Two axes · The nine alignments · Evil characters · Personality traits · Alignment and the rules

Two axes

Alignment has two parts. The first is morality: good, evil or neutral. The second is the attitude toward order and society: lawful, chaotic or neutral. Together they make nine combinations. The descriptions below show typical behavior; a particular character may stray from it, and few follow their alignment all the time.

The nine alignments

Lawful Good (LG)
Does the right thing as expected by society. For example, someone who fights injustice and protects the innocent without hesitation.
Neutral Good (NG)
Does the best they can to help others, working within rules but not feeling bound by them. For example, a kind person who helps others according to their needs.
Chaotic Good (CG)
Acts as their conscience directs, with little regard for what others expect. For example, a rebel who robs a cruel baron’s tax collectors and gives the money to the poor.
Lawful Neutral (LN)
Acts in accordance with law, tradition or a personal code. For example, someone who follows a disciplined routine and isn’t swayed by the pleas of the needy or the temptations of evil.
Neutral (N)
Prefers to avoid moral questions, doesn’t take sides and does what seems best at the time. For example, someone bored by moral debate.
Chaotic Neutral (CN)
Follows their whims, valuing personal freedom above all else. For example, a scoundrel who wanders and lives by their wits.
Lawful Evil (LE)
Methodically takes what they want within the limits of a code of tradition, loyalty or order. For example, an aristocrat who exploits citizens and schemes for power.
Neutral Evil (NE)
Does whatever they can get away with, untroubled by the harm they cause. For example, a criminal who robs and kills as they please.
Chaotic Evil (CE)
Acts with arbitrary violence, spurred by hatred or bloodlust. For example, a villain pursuing schemes of vengeance and havoc.

Evil characters

The rules say it plainly: D&D assumes that player characters aren’t of an evil alignment — check with your DM before making an evil character.

Personality traits by alignment

Alignment helps you come up with a personality: take both parts of your alignment and choose (or roll a d4) one trait for each.

Alignment and the rules

Almost no game rules depend on alignment. The Detect Evil and Good and Protection from Evil and Good spells work by creature type (aberrations, celestials, fiends and so on), not by alignment.
