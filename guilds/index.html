Guilds of Ravnica and artisan guilds

The ten guilds of Ravnica from Guildmasters' Guide to Ravnica: what each does, its colors, background and guild spells. Below are the artisan and merchant guilds for the Guild Artisan background.

Ravnica · The ten guilds · Guild spells · Renown · Artisan guilds

Ravnica

Ravnica is a city-world from the Magic: The Gathering multiverse: a vast metropolis where power is divided among ten guilds. Each guild combines two of the five colors of magic and oversees its part of city life; those who belong to none are called guildless. The builder’s peoples of Ravnica are the loxodons, vedalken and Simic hybrids, as well as humans, elves, goblins, centaurs, minotaurs and others.

Every guild background’s equipment includes the guild’s insignia — the sign by which its members are recognized.

Renown

A character’s standing in a guild is reflected by renown: it grows as the character carries out the guild’s missions and acts in its interests. As renown grows, they rise in the hierarchy and get more serious assignments — a Golgari agent, for example, might join the Ochran, who are entrusted with theft, assassination and the protection of the guild’s important figures.

Artisan and merchant guilds

The Guild Artisan background from the 2014 Player’s Handbook isn’t tied to Ravnica. Guilds are usually founded in cities where several masters practice the same trade, but a guild can also be a network of artisans across villages. Choose the specialty with your DM or roll a d20:

  1. Alchemists and apothecaries
  2. Armorers, locksmiths, and finesmiths
  3. Brewers, distillers, and vintners
  4. Calligraphers, scribes, and scriveners
  5. Carpenters, roofers, and plasterers
  6. Cartographers, surveyors, and chart-makers
  7. Cobblers and shoemakers
  8. Cooks and bakers
  9. Glassblowers and glaziers
  10. Jewelers and gemcutters
  11. Leatherworkers, skinners, and tanners
  12. Masons and stonecutters
  13. Painters, limners, and sign-makers
  14. Potters and tile-makers
  15. Shipwrights and sailmakers
  16. Smiths and metal-forgers
  17. Tinkers, pewterers, and casters
  18. Wagon-makers and wheelwrights
  19. Weavers and dyers
  20. Woodcarvers, coopers, and bowyers

A guild member knows their trade and the basics of commerce. Fellow guild members will provide lodging and food if needed and even pay for a funeral; the guild helps in court and puts you in touch with influential people, and in return members pay dues.

Variant: guild merchant

A character can belong not to an artisan guild but to a guild of merchants, caravan masters and shopkeepers: they make nothing themselves but buy and sell the goods or raw materials of others. Instead of proficiency with artisan’s tools they can be proficient with navigator’s tools or an additional language, and instead of tools they can start with a mule and a cart.
