D&D gods: pantheons and deities

114 deities from the 2014 Player’s Handbook: the gods of the Forgotten Realms, nonhuman deities and fantasy versions of the Celtic, Greek, Egyptian and Norse pantheons — with alignment, suggested cleric domains and holy symbol. How faith works and who needs a god: Religion in D&D.

The Forgotten Realms · Nonhuman deities · The Celtic pantheon · The Greek pantheon · The Egyptian pantheon · The Norse pantheon · Gods by domain

Your DM decides which gods exist in the campaign. The domains in the tables are suggestions: a cleric chooses a domain tied to their deity. The Death domain is described in the Dungeon Master’s Guide; its clerics are usually evil nonplayer characters, so discuss serving a god of death with your DM. The gods of Theros are on the Gods of Theros page.

The Forgotten Realms

Dozens of gods are worshiped across Faerûn: at least thirty are widely known, and many more by individual tribes, cults and sects. Many people pray to different gods as the occasion calls: to Sune for luck in love, to Waukeen before going to market, to Talos to calm a storm.

Nonhuman deities

Gods closely tied to nonhuman peoples are worshiped on many worlds, though in different ways; the peoples of the Forgotten Realms and Greyhawk share them. Many peoples have entire pantheons: dwarves, for example, revere not only Moradin but also his wife Berronar Truesilver and their offspring — Abbathor, Clangeddin Silverbeard, Dumathoin, Haela Brightaxe and others. The Egyptian pantheon

A young dynasty of an ancient divine family, keepers of Ma’at — the order of truth, justice and law. Unusually, there are three gods of death of different alignments: Anubis, Set and Nephthys — so a cleric of Anubis or Nephthys isn’t necessarily a villain.

Gods by domain

In each domain’s description, the 2014 Player’s Handbook gives example gods from the Forgotten Realms, Greyhawk, Dragonlance and Eberron, as well as from the ancient pantheons.

Knowledge
Oghma, Boccob, Gilean, Aureon, Thoth; gods of smiths and inventors — Gond, Reorx, Onatar, Moradin, Hephaestus, Goibhniu.
Life
Almost any non-evil deity. Especially gods of agriculture (Chauntea, Arawai, Demeter), the sun (Lathander, Pelor, Re-Horakhty), healing and endurance (Ilmater, Mishakal, Apollo, Diancecht), home and community (Hestia, Hathor, Boldrei).
Light
Helm, Lathander, Pholtus, Branchala, the Silver Flame, Belenus, Apollo, Re-Horakhty.
Nature
Gods of the deep forests (Silvanus, Obad-Hai, Chislev, Balinor, Pan) and friendly deities of springs and groves (Eldath).
Tempest
Talos, Umberlee, Kord, Zeboim, the Devourer, Zeus, Thor.
Trickery
Tymora, Beshaba, Olidammara, the Traveler, Garl Glittergold, Loki.
War
Gods of honor and chivalry (Torm, Heironeous, Kiri-Jolith), destruction and pillage (Erythnul, the Fury, Gruumsh, Ares), conquest (Bane, Hextor, Maglubiyet) and neutral gods of war (Tempus, Nike, Nuada).
