D&D 2024 multiclassing: prerequisites, proficiencies, spell slots

Multiclassing means having levels in several classes. When you gain a level, you can take it in a different class instead of your current one: for example, a level 5 wizard / level 1 fighter is a level 6 character. It lets you build concepts no single class covers, but you reach powerful high-level features more slowly.

Prerequisites

To take a new class you need a score of 13 or higher in the primary ability of both the new class and all your current classes. A barbarian who wants to become a druid needs Strength 13 and Wisdom 13.

Proficiencies gained are what you get from your first level in a class that isn’t your starting one: only some of its starting proficiencies. You don’t get the class’s starting equipment.

How it works

Experience and Proficiency Bonus
Both are based on your total character level, not your level in a class. A level 3 fighter / level 2 rogue is a level 5 character with a +3 Proficiency Bonus.
Hit Points and Hit Dice
Only your very first class gives the maximum level 1 Hit Points. Levels in a new class add Hit Points as usual for higher levels. Hit Dice from different classes form one pool but are tracked separately by type (d8 and d10).
Extra Attack
Extra Attack from several classes doesn’t stack: you make more than two attacks only if a feature says so (like a level 11 fighter’s).
Unarmored Defense and AC
If you have several ways to calculate AC (monk and sorcerer, barbarian and monk), only one applies, of your choice.

Spellcasting and spell slots

The spells you know and prepare are determined for each class separately, as if it were your only class; each spell uses its own class’s spellcasting ability. Cantrips scale with your total character level.

Spell slots are shared. Add up your Bard, Cleric, Druid, Sorcerer and Wizard levels, half your Paladin and Ranger levels (rounded up) and one third of your Fighter or Rogue levels if you have the Eldritch Knight or Arcane Trickster subclass (rounded down). Look up the total in the table.

The table may give you slots higher than the spells you have: you can use them to cast lower-level spells, upcast if the spell allows. Warlock Pact Magic slots and regular slots can be spent on spells from both classes.
