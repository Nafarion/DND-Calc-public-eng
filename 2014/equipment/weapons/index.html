D&D 2014 weapons

All equipment · Weapons · Armor and shields · Tools · Adventuring gear and packs · Mounts and vehicles · Services and lifestyle

Weapons are Simple (almost everyone is proficient) or Martial (fighters, barbarians, paladins, rangers), and Melee or Ranged. Proficiency adds your Proficiency Bonus to the attack roll; you can attack without it, just without the bonus.

A melee attack uses Strength and a ranged attack uses Dexterity; with a Finesse weapon you choose either. Follow the links in the table for what each property means.

Special weapon rules

Lance
Special: you have disadvantage when you use a lance to attack a target within 5 feet of you; a lance requires two hands to wield when you aren't mounted.
Musket
An optional rule from the Dungeon Master’s Guide: the DM decides whether it’s in the campaign. Proficiency is also up to the DM (or the Gunner feat). Ammunition: bullets (10).
Pistol
An optional rule from the Dungeon Master’s Guide: the DM decides whether it’s in the campaign. Proficiency is also up to the DM (or the Gunner feat). Ammunition: bullets (10).
Net
A Large or smaller creature hit by a net is restrained until it frees itself (a DC 10 Strength check as an action, or 5 slashing damage to the net, AC 10). It has no effect on formless or Huge creatures. You can attack with a net only once per action, bonus action or reaction.
Double-Bladed Scimitar
When you take the Attack action and attack with it, you can strike with the other end as a bonus action: 1d4 slashing damage.
Hoopak
With a melee attack, the ammunition property doesn't apply. It can be fired as a martial ranged weapon: the ammunition is sling bullets, 1d4 bludgeoning damage.
Hooked Shortspear
On a hit, instead of dealing damage, you can hook the target: Strength saving throw (DC 8 + Strength modifier + proficiency bonus), or it falls prone. The DM sets the price.
Light Repeating Crossbow
A magazine of six bolts: it reloads itself after a shot until the magazine is empty; loading a magazine takes an action. It doesn't have the loading property. The DM sets the price.

Improvised weapons

Fighting with a table leg, a bottle or a frying pan uses an improvised weapon. If the object resembles a real weapon, the DM can treat it as that weapon (a table leg works like a club). Otherwise it deals 1d4 damage of a fitting type, without your Proficiency Bonus; you can throw it with a range of 20/60 ft. Hitting with a ranged weapon in melee or throwing a melee weapon without the Thrown property is also an improvised attack.

Silvered weapons

Some monsters resist nonmagical weapons but not silver. Silvering one weapon or ten pieces of ammunition costs 100 gp.

Source: the 2014 Player’s Handbook (free Basic Rules on D&D Beyond); supplement items are marked with their book. Costs and weights match the D20Hero builder.
