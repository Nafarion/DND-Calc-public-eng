Faerûn: regions, languages, calendar and gods

A short guide to Faerûn for players using the 2024 rules, based on Forgotten Realms: Heroes of Faerûn. It helps you decide where your character comes from, which language they speak and which god they pray to.

What is Faerûn · Regions · Languages · Calendar · Coins · Gods

What is Faerûn

The Forgotten Realms is the best-known world of D&D: many official adventures and video games, such as Baldur’s Gate, take place there. Faerûn is a continent of that world on the planet Toril, between the Trackless Sea to the west and the Sunrise Mountains to the east.

Humans are the most numerous people of Faerûn, but dwarves, elves, halflings, gnomes, dragonborn, goliaths, orcs, tieflings and many others live alongside them. Except in the most remote places, people are used to neighbors of other kinds, so your character can be anyone.

How to use this page: pick the region your character comes from — it suggests a language, a background and a personality. If you play a cleric or paladin, see the table of gods. The calendar is handy for the DM and for anyone keeping an adventure journal.

The ten regions of Faerûn

The book divides Faerûn into ten regions with a shared culture. Each comes with a background from the same book that suits its natives best — but you don’t have to take it: the Chondathan Freebooter, for example, fits any pirate.

The Calendar of Harptos

Nearly all of Faerûn keeps time with the Calendar of Harptos. A year has 365 days: 12 months of 30 days and five holidays between the months. A month is three tendays; individual days have no names — people say “first day,” “second day,” and so on. A date is given as a number and month (“1 Mirtul,” “27 Uktar”) or relative to a holiday (“three days past Greengrass”). Every four years a leap day, Shieldmeet, follows Midsummer.

Midwinter
The midpoint of the cold season: nobles make or renew alliances, commoners celebrate that the worst days are past.
Greengrass
The spring festival: fresh flowers are given to the gods or spread over the fields for a good harvest.
Midsummer
Feasting, carousing and betrothals; a storm that night is seen as a bad omen.
Shieldmeet
Once every four years: rulers and subjects speak openly, pacts and treaties are made, tournaments are held.
Highharvestide
Thanks are given to Chauntea for the harvest; many set out on long journeys before winter closes the passes and harbors.
Feast of the Moon
Before winter, people honor their ancestors and the fallen and share stories and legends.

Hours and minutes exist mostly where wealthy people own clocks, and those are unreliable. People usually say “midmorning” or “near sunset,” and where a temple or tower rings the hours, “I’ll meet you at seven bells.”

Coins

The rules price things in copper, silver, electrum, gold and platinum pieces, and these are accepted almost everywhere in Faerûn. But major realms mint their own coins with their own names — from the cheapest to the most valuable:

  • Amn: fander, taran, centaur, danter, roldon
  • Calimshan: unarche, decarche, centarche, bicenta, kilarche
  • Cormyr: thumb, falcon, blue eye, golden lion, tricrown
  • Sembia: steelpence (an iron coin), hawk, blue eye, noble
  • Silverymoon: glint, shield, sword, dragon, unicorn
  • Waterdeep: nib, shard, sambar, dragon, sun

The gods of Faerûn

People in Faerûn worship many gods at once: a farmer prays to Chauntea for a good harvest and placates Umberlee before a voyage. A cleric or paladin usually picks one patron god. In the 2024 rules a god grants no features and doesn’t restrict a cleric’s domain — it’s part of your character’s story to discuss with the DM.
