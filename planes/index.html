D&D planes of existence: the Great Wheel and the multiverse

How the D&D multiverse works: the Material Plane and its echoes, the Ethereal and Astral Planes, the elemental planes, the Upper and Lower Planes of the Great Wheel and the city of Sigil. Which races, subclasses and backgrounds are tied to the planes, and how to get there.

How the multiverse works

The D&D multiverse is a host of worlds and other dimensions of reality — the planes of existence. Every game world lies on the Material Plane; the other planes aren’t just other worlds but different states of being, governed by spiritual and elemental principles. There lie the realms of the elements, the domains of thought and alignment, the homes of demons and angels, the abodes of the gods.

The familiar model is the Great Wheel: in the center, the Material Plane and its echoes; around it, the elemental Inner Planes, reached through the Ethereal Plane; through the Astral Plane one travels to the Outer Planes, arranged in a circle of alignments. Your DM decides how the multiverse works in a particular campaign.

The Material Plane

The Material Plane is where the philosophical and elemental forces of the other planes collide in the colorful lives of mortals. All fantasy worlds lie on it, including the Forgotten Realms, Greyhawk, Eberron and Krynn, and most campaigns begin there. The rest of the multiverse is described in relation to it.

The Feywild and the Shadowfell

The Material Plane has echoes — planes that mirror its shape. The Feywild is a bright, ever-changing world of the fey. The Shadowfell is a bleak, colorless reflection; the shadar-kai, servants of the Raven Queen, come from there.

  • Feywild: the Eladrin, Fairy, Harengon and Satyr races; the Archfey (2014, 2024) warlock patron; the Fey Wanderer ranger and the Circle of Dreams druid; the Feylost and Witchlight Hand backgrounds.
  • Shadowfell: the Shadar-Kai race; the Shadow Magic sorcerer and the warlock with the The Hexblade patron.

The Ethereal and Astral Planes

The Ethereal and Astral Planes are nearly empty and serve above all as roads between other planes. The Ethereal Plane is like a misty ocean. Its shores — the Border Ethereal — overlap the Material and Inner Planes: every place there has an ethereal counterpart. Some creatures can see into the Border Ethereal, and the See Invisibility and True Seeing spells grant that ability. Its depths, the Deep Ethereal, are a region of swirling colored mists.

The Astral Plane is the realm of thought and dream, a vast silvery sea that looks the same above and below. Travelers cross it as disembodied souls to reach the divine and demonic Outer Planes. The githyanki live here, the astral elves come from here, and a character with the Astral Drifter background wandered the Astral Sea.

The elemental planes

The Inner Planes surround the Material Plane and provide the raw stuff all worlds are made of. The four elemental planes — Air, Earth, Fire and Water — ring the Material Plane amid the churning Elemental Chaos. At their inner edges, closer to the Material Plane, the elements mix as in the ordinary world; farther out they become pure and hostile, and at the farthest reaches they dissolve into an endless clash of energies. The City of Brass stands on the Elemental Plane of Fire.

  • Genasi carry the power of one of the elemental planes.
  • The The Genie warlock patron is a djinni, dao, efreeti or marid — a noble genie of one of the elemental planes.

The Outer Planes

If the Inner Planes are the matter and energy of the multiverse, the Outer Planes are its thought and purpose. They are called the divine or spiritual planes: the gods dwell here. Their appearance is deceptive — mighty powers can reshape them at will, and distance means almost nothing here. The best-known Outer Planes are the sixteen planes matching the eight alignments (excluding neutral) and the shades between them, and the Outlands at the center of the Great Wheel.

Planes with an element of good are called the Upper Planes: celestials such as angels and pegasi live there. Planes with an element of evil are the Lower Planes: fiends such as demons and devils live there. A plane’s alignment is its essence: a good creature in Elysium (a neutral good Upper Plane) feels in harmony with it, and an evil one feels out of place.

The Upper and Lower Planes in the builder

  • Aasimar (2014, 2024) carry a spark of the Upper Planes; the Celestial (2014, 2024) warlock patron is a being of the Upper Planes.
  • Tieflings (2014, 2024) are bound by blood to the fiends of the Lower Planes.
  • The Fiend (2014, 2024) warlock patron is a lord of the Lower Planes; who it might be: Archdevils and demon lords.

Demiplanes

Demiplanes are small spaces with their own rules that don’t fit into any other plane. They are created by spells such as Demiplane, by the will of a deity or another mighty power; there are also natural demiplanes — folds of reality cut off from the rest of the multiverse. A demiplane is entered through the single point where it touches another plane; Gate is more reliable than Plane Shift if the caster knows about the demiplane. The Domains of Dread of Ravenloft are demiplanes too — the home of dhampirs, hexbloods and the reborn.

Sigil, the Outlands and the factions

At the center of the Outlands stands Sigil, the City of Doors — the heart of the Planescape setting. Its portals lead to every corner of the multiverse, and the mysterious Lady of Pain rules it. Along the edge of the Outlands stand the gate-towns with portals to the Outer Planes.

The Planescape backgrounds — Planar Philosopher and Gate Warden — grant the Scion of the Outer Planes feat: a bond with a plane gives a damage resistance and a cantrip.

  • Chaotic Outer Plane: cantrip Minor Illusion
  • Evil Outer Plane: cantrip Chill Touch
  • Good Outer Plane: cantrip Sacred Flame
  • Lawful Outer Plane: cantrip Guidance
  • Outlands: cantrip Mage Hand

Twelve factions are known in the City of Doors — philosophical societies, each with its own view of the multiverse. A planar philosopher belongs to one of them and gains their faction’s skill (or any skill of their choice).

How to reach another plane

There are two ways to reach another plane: a spell or a portal. Plane Shift and Gate carry you straight to any plane with varying accuracy; Etherealness takes you to the Ethereal Plane and from there to the planes it touches, such as the elemental ones; Astral Projection sends astral bodies across the Astral Plane to the Outer Planes.

A portal is a fixed connection between a particular place on one plane and a particular place on another. Some portals look like a door, a window or a misty passage; others are whole places: circles of standing stones, towers, ships or cities that exist on several planes at once. There are also vortices linking an elemental plane to a similar place on the Material Plane: a volcano’s heart to the Plane of Fire, the ocean depths to the Plane of Water.

The Banishment, Planar Ally and Planar Binding spells are tied to the planes as well. Travel between the worlds of the Material Plane is the theme of the Horizon Walker ranger subclass.
