How to create a D&D character (2014 rules)

Step by step for first-time players: what to choose, in what order and what follows from it. The D20Hero builder walks through the same steps and does all the math for you.

Build a character in the builder (2014 rules) →

Before you start

Talk to your DM: which world the game is set in, which level you start at, which books are allowed and how to generate ability scores. Find out what the other players are making — it helps when the party has someone to take hits, someone to heal and someone who handles magic or traps.

The core of a character is six abilities: Strength, Dexterity, Constitution, Intelligence, Wisdom and Charisma. At level 1 their scores are usually 8 to 15. A score gives a modifier: 10–11 is +0, each 2 points above adds +1 (12–13 → +1, 14–15 → +2), each 2 points below is −1 (8–9 → −1). You add the modifier to d20 rolls — ability checks, saving throws and attacks.

Step 1. Race

Your race is your character’s people: human, elf, dwarf, halfling and others. In the 2014 rules your race gives ability score bonuses (usually +2 to one and +1 to another), size, speed, languages and special traits. Many races have subraces. Races →

Step 2. Class

Your class is your hero’s calling. It sets Hit Points and Hit Dice, armor, weapon and skill proficiencies, saving throws and features by level. Make the class’s primary ability your highest. Your proficiency bonus at level 1 is +2. All classes →

Step 3. Ability scores

There are three methods (your DM decides which to use):

Standard array
The scores 15, 14, 13, 12, 10, 8 — assign them to your abilities. The simplest and fairest method.
Point buy
27 points to buy scores from 8 to 15: 8 costs 0 points, 13 costs 5, 14 costs 7, 15 costs 9.
Rolling
Roll 4d6 six times and add the three highest dice each time. More random: you might come out ahead or behind.

Then add your racial bonuses. Put the highest score in your class’s primary ability and the next in Constitution (Hit Points) or whatever else your class needs.

Step 4. Describe your character

Choose a name, appearance, personality and alignment. In the 2014 rules a background gives two skills, tools or languages, a special feature (such as guild contacts) and equipment, plus tables of personality traits, ideals, bonds and flaws for roleplaying. Backgrounds → · Alignment →

Step 5. Equipment and numbers

Hit Points
At level 1 — the maximum of your class’s Hit Die plus your Constitution modifier (fighter: 10 + Con). Each level after — a roll of the die or its average plus Constitution.
Armor Class (AC)
How hard you are to hit. Without armor — 10 + Dexterity; armor has its own formula, a shield adds +2.
Weapon attacks
Attack roll: d20 + modifier (Strength for melee, Dexterity for ranged and Finesse weapons) + proficiency bonus if you’re proficient. Damage — the weapon’s die + the same modifier.
Proficiency bonus
+2 at levels 1–4, rising to +6 by level 17. You add it to everything you’re proficient in: skills, class saving throws, weapons, spells.
Equipment
Your class and background give starting equipment; alternatively take gold and buy everything yourself.

What next

Adventures earn experience, or the DM simply grants levels. Each level adds Hit Points, new features and spells, and at levels 4, 8, 12, 16 and 19 — an ability score increase or a feat. Tested ready-made builds for every class are in the class guides. Rules you’ll need in your first game: combat, rests and travel, spellcasting.

Source: the 2014 Player’s Handbook (free Basic Rules on D&D Beyond). A short summary in our own words — see the book for exact wording.
